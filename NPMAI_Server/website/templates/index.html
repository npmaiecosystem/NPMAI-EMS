THE PHYSICS

A loose connection doesn't stay the same size. It grows itself.

When a wire terminal loosens even slightly, electricity has to fight harder to cross the gap. That resistance turns into heat. The heat expands and contracts the metal every time a load switches on and off — and each cycle loosens the joint a little more.

This is why the same joint can run for weeks or months before it's hot enough to smell, spark, or trip a breaker. By the time it's obvious, it's already been dangerous for a long time. NPMAI-PEMS is built around catching the early, invisible part of this cycle — not the end of it.

HOW WE MONITOR & PREDICT

Not a smoke detector. A resistance detector.

Here's what actually happens inside the system — in plain terms, not engineering flowcharts.

CH.01

We measure two things at every joint

A clamp reads the current flowing through the wire. A thermal camera reads the joint's temperature against the panel's normal air temperature nearby. Neither number alone means much — a joint under heavy load is naturally warmer, and that's normal.

CH.02

We calculate what "normal" looks like for that exact joint

Every joint has its own healthy resistance — a thick busbar and a small lighting circuit run differently, and that's fine. We spend the first week quietly learning each joint's own baseline before judging anything against it. A joint is only ever compared to itself.

CH.03

We track the gap over time, not the temperature in the moment

The number that matters is the difference between "how hot this joint should be right now, given the current load" and "how hot it actually is." A healthy joint's gap stays flat for months. A degrading joint's gap climbs — slowly at first, then faster — and we track that climb using the same statistical method industrial plants use to catch slow process drift before it becomes a failure.

CH.04

We tell you before it's an emergency, not after

Once a joint's drift is real and sustained — not just one warm afternoon — the system estimates how many days remain before it reaches a genuinely unsafe resistance, and writes you a plain-language report explaining which joint, why, and what to do. Three scheduled check-ins a day, plus an immediate alert the moment something crosses a real threshold.
